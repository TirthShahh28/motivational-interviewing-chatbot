# Handling Defensiveness and Resistance

## Understanding Resistance

Resistance is a normal response when people feel their autonomy is threatened. It's not something to overcome - it's information about the interaction.

### Common Forms of Defensiveness:

1. **Denial**: "I don't have a problem with drinking."
2. **Minimization**: "I only drink a little - it's not that bad."
3. **Rationalization**: "I drink because of stress at work. Anyone would."
4. **Projection**: "My partner is the one with the problem, not me."
5. **Hostility**: "Why are you attacking me? Mind your own business."

## Key Principle: Roll With Resistance

Never argue or confront. Resistance met with force creates more resistance. Instead:

- Accept the resistance as valid from their perspective
- Avoid the "righting reflex" (the urge to fix or correct)
- Shift focus or approach
- Emphasize autonomy

## Strategies for High Defensiveness

### Simple Reflection

Reflect exactly what they said without adding interpretation.

User: "I don't have a drinking problem."
Response: "You don't see it as a problem."

### Amplified Reflection

Slightly overstate their position (carefully, without sarcasm).

User: "I can stop whenever I want."
Response: "You feel completely in control of it."

### Double-Sided Reflection

Acknowledge both sides of their ambivalence.

User: "Everyone keeps telling me to quit but I don't want to."
Response: "On one hand, people are expressing concern, and on the other, you're not ready to make changes - and that's your decision to make."

### Emphasize Autonomy

Remind them that the choice is theirs.

- "It's completely up to you."
- "You're the expert on your own life."
- "No one can make this decision for you."
- "Whatever you decide, it's your choice."

### Reframe

Offer a different interpretation without arguing.

User: "My wife nags me constantly about drinking."
Response: "She seems to care deeply about you."

### Shift Focus

Move away from the sticking point.

User: "I'm not an alcoholic!"
Response: "Labels aren't really important here. I'm more interested in what you've noticed about how drinking fits into your life."

## What NOT to Do

- Don't argue or try to convince
- Don't label them ("You're in denial")
- Don't give unsolicited advice
- Don't use scare tactics
- Don't express frustration
- Don't take it personally
- Don't raise your voice or match their intensity
- Don't threaten consequences
