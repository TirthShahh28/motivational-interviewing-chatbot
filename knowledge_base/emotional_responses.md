# Responding to Different Emotional States

## Frustration

### Signs

- Short, clipped responses
- Sighing, expressions like "ugh" or "whatever"
- Statements about being tired of something
- Feeling unheard or misunderstood

### Strategies

- Validate the frustration first
- Name the emotion explicitly
- Explore what's underneath
- Don't rush or push

### Example Responses

- "It sounds really frustrating."
- "You've been dealing with a lot."
- "It makes sense that you'd feel worn down by this."

## Anxiety

### Signs

- "What if" questions
- Expressing worry about the future
- Uncertainty about change
- Fear of failure or unknown

### Strategies

- Normalize anxiety about change
- Break things into smaller steps
- Focus on what's in their control
- Provide reassurance without minimizing

### Example Responses

- "Change can feel scary - that's completely normal."
- "What would the smallest possible step look like?"
- "You don't have to figure it all out right now."

## Sadness / Hopelessness

### Signs

- Expressions of defeat ("What's the point?")
- Looking back with regret
- Believing change is impossible
- Withdrawn, low energy

### Strategies

- Sit with them emotionally - don't rush to fix
- Acknowledge the pain
- Gently explore exceptions (times things were different)
- Look for any spark of hope to nurture

### Example Responses

- "You're carrying a lot of weight right now."
- "It sounds like you've been feeling pretty low."
- "Even in dark times, you reached out today. That takes something."

## Anger

### Signs

- Raised intensity
- Blaming language
- Defensive or attacking posture
- Feeling wronged or treated unfairly

### Strategies

- Stay calm - do not match their energy
- Validate the feeling behind the anger
- Don't take it personally
- Give space if needed

### Example Responses

- "I can hear how angry you are about this."
- "It sounds like you feel really let down."
- "It's okay to be upset. I'm still here."

## Hope / Openness

### Signs

- Asking questions about change
- Expressing interest in alternatives
- Using language like "maybe I could..."
- Weighing pros and cons

### Strategies

- Support and encourage without pushing
- Explore the hope further
- Ask about their vision for change
- Affirm their strength

### Example Responses

- "What would life look like if things were different?"
- "It sounds like a part of you is curious about change."
- "You're starting to see some possibilities - that's important."

## Ambivalence

### Signs

- "Yeah, but..." statements
- Going back and forth
- Seeing both sides
- Feeling stuck

### Strategies

- This is normal and healthy! Don't rush them
- Use double-sided reflections
- Explore both sides without pushing toward one
- Summarize their ambivalence back to them

### Example Responses

- "You're seeing both the pros and cons here."
- "Part of you wants to change, and part of you isn't sure."
- "It sounds like you're weighing some important things."
