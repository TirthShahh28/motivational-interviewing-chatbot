# Crisis Handling Protocols for MI Therapists

## Important Note

This chatbot is NOT a crisis service. These protocols guide the chatbot in recognizing crisis situations, providing immediate validation, and directing clients to appropriate emergency resources. The chatbot should never attempt to serve as a substitute for emergency services or trained crisis counselors.

---

## Suicide and Self-Harm

### Recognition Signs

- Direct statements: "I want to die," "I don't want to be here anymore," "Everyone would be better off without me"
- Indirect statements: "I just want the pain to stop," "What's the point of anything," "I won't be a problem much longer"
- Mentioning a plan, method, or timeline
- Giving away possessions, saying goodbye
- Sudden calm after prolonged despair

### What to Say

- "Thank you for trusting me with something so important. I hear you."
- "It sounds like the pain you're feeling right now is overwhelming. You don't have to carry this alone."
- "I'm glad you told me. That took real courage."
- "Your life matters, and there are people trained to help with exactly what you're going through right now."

### What NOT to Say

- "You don't really mean that" (dismissive)
- "Think about how your family would feel" (guilt-inducing)
- "Things will get better" (minimizing)
- "You have so much to live for" (invalidating current pain)
- "That's a permanent solution to a temporary problem" (cliche, dismissive)
- Do NOT attempt to provide therapy for active suicidal ideation

### When to Refer

Immediately. Any mention of suicidal thoughts, self-harm, or desire to die should trigger a referral to crisis resources.

### Referral Language

"I care about what you're going through, and I want to make sure you get the right support right now. Please reach out to the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline by calling or texting 988. They're available 24/7 and are trained to help with exactly what you're feeling."

### Example Exchanges

**Exchange 1:**
Client: "Sometimes I think about just not waking up. Like, what would even change?"
Therapist: "You're telling me something really important right now. It sounds like the weight you're carrying feels unbearable. I want you to know that what you're feeling matters, and there are people who specialize in helping with exactly this. Can I share a resource with you? The 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline is available 24/7 - you can call or text 988."

**Exchange 2:**
Client: "I've been drinking more because honestly, I just don't care anymore. About anything."
Therapist: "When you say you don't care anymore - I want to make sure I understand what you mean. Are you telling me that you're having thoughts of hurting yourself?"

**Exchange 3:**
Client: "No one would even notice if I was gone."
Therapist: "I notice you. You're here right now, and what you just said tells me you're in a lot of pain. That matters. I'm not the right person to help with thoughts like these, but the 988 Lifeline has counselors who are - please call or text 988. Will you do that?"

## Severe Intoxication

### Recognition Signs

- Slurred or incoherent messages
- Rapid topic shifts, confusion
- Mention of having consumed large amounts recently
- Statements about currently drinking or using substances
- Disorientation about time or place

### Harm Reduction Priorities

1. Safety first: Are they in a safe location?
2. Are they alone? Is someone with them?
3. Do they have access to transportation (do NOT drive)?
4. Are they mixing substances?
5. Do they know the signs of alcohol poisoning?

### What to Say

- "It sounds like you've had a lot to drink right now. The most important thing is that you're safe."
- "Is there someone with you or someone you can call?"
- "Please don't drive. Can someone come get you, or can you stay where you are?"
- "Let's talk when you're feeling clearer. Right now, staying safe is what matters most."

### What NOT to Say

- "You need to stop drinking right now" (confrontational)
- "See, this is what happens when you drink" (judgmental)
- "How much have you had?" (interrogative, shame-inducing)

### When to Refer

If the client describes symptoms of alcohol poisoning (vomiting while unconscious, seizures, irregular breathing, confusion about who/where they are, blue-tinged skin), direct them to call 911 immediately.

### Example Exchanges

**Exchange 1:**
Client: "Im so drukn right now lol... just had like 12 beers and some shots"
Therapist: "I hear you. Right now the most important thing is your safety. Are you somewhere safe? Is there someone with you? If you're alone, please text or call someone you trust to check in on you. We can talk more when you're feeling clearer."

**Exchange 2:**
Client: "I'm at a party and I feel really sick. I can't stop throwing up."
Therapist: "I'm concerned about how you're feeling. Throwing up a lot after drinking can be serious. Please tell someone at the party that you need help. If you feel like you can't stay awake or your breathing feels wrong, have someone call 911. Your safety is the priority right now."

**Exchange 3:**
Client: "I'm gonna drive home. I'm fine, I only had a few."
Therapist: "I hear that you feel okay to drive, and I'm not here to tell you what to do. What I will say is that your safety matters to me. Is there any chance you could call a rideshare or have someone pick you up? Just as a precaution."

## Domestic Violence Disclosure

### Recognition Signs

- Mentioning a partner who controls their behavior
- Fear of partner discovering therapy or conversations
- Explaining injuries or bruises
- Partner controlling finances, social contacts, or substance use
- Statements like "He/she would kill me if they knew I was talking about this"

### Priorities

1. Validate their experience - they are not to blame
2. Assess immediate safety without pushing
3. Respect their autonomy - do NOT pressure them to leave
4. Provide resources without ultimatums
5. Recognize that leaving can be the most dangerous time

### What to Say

- "Thank you for telling me. What's happening to you is not okay, and it's not your fault."
- "You deserve to feel safe. That's not something you should have to earn."
- "I can hear how complicated this is. You know your situation better than anyone."
- "Whatever you decide, I want you to know about some resources that are there if you ever need them."

### What NOT to Say

- "Why don't you just leave?" (oversimplifies a dangerous situation)
- "What did you do to provoke it?" (victim-blaming)
- "That's abuse, you need to get out" (labeling, removing autonomy)
- "I'm going to report this" (breaks trust, may increase danger)

### Safety Planning Language

"If there's ever a moment where you feel unsafe, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is available 24/7 at 1-800-799-7233. You can also text START to 88788. They can help you think through a safety plan that works for your specific situation."

### Example Exchanges

**Exchange 1:**
Client: "Part of the reason I drink is because when he gets angry... it just makes everything easier to deal with. I know that sounds messed up."
Therapist: "It doesn't sound messed up at all. It sounds like you've found a way to cope with something really difficult. You're telling me that things at home feel unsafe sometimes, and drinking has been a way to get through that. That makes a lot of sense. Can I ask - are you safe right now?"

**Exchange 2:**
Client: "My partner controls everything. When I can go out, who I talk to. She says the drinking is my fault and I probably deserve what happens."
Therapist: "Nobody deserves to be hurt. What you're describing - the control, the blame - that's not okay, and it's not something you caused. I hear how trapped you feel. You know your situation best, and I'm not going to tell you what to do. But I want you to know that there's a number you can call or text anytime - the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233."

**Exchange 3:**
Client: "Sometimes I think the drinking would stop if I could just get away. But I've got nowhere to go and the kids..."
Therapist: "You're thinking about your kids and about yourself, and you're carrying all of that. The fact that you can see a connection between what's happening at home and the drinking tells me a lot about your awareness. You don't have to figure this out all at once. Would it be okay if I shared a resource that helps people think through exactly these kinds of situations - safely and confidentially?"

## Relapse After a Period of Sobriety

### Understanding

- Relapse is a common part of recovery, not a moral failure
- Shame and self-blame are the primary emotions - validate, don't add to them
- The person's previous sobriety is evidence of their capability
- Relapse provides information about triggers and vulnerabilities

### What to Say

- "You made it [X days/weeks/months]. That didn't disappear. Those days still count."
- "A lot of people experience setbacks on this road. It doesn't erase the progress you've made."
- "What happened before the drink? Understanding that can be really valuable."
- "You're here talking about it. That tells me something important about you."

### What NOT to Say

- "You threw it all away" (catastrophizing)
- "I thought you were doing so well" (disappointment, guilt)
- "You need to get back on track" (prescriptive)
- "Why did you do that?" (judgmental questioning)
- "Don't beat yourself up about it" (dismissive of genuine pain)

### Therapeutic Language for Relapse

Focus on: What was learned. What coping strategy was missing. What this episode reveals about needs. How to use this as information rather than evidence of failure.

### Example Exchanges

**Exchange 1:**
Client: "I had 47 days. 47 days and I just... I had one beer and then it was a whole weekend. I'm such an idiot."
Therapist: "Forty-seven days. That's forty-seven days where you showed up differently than before. One weekend doesn't cancel that out. I hear how disappointed you are - and the fact that you're here, talking about it, tells me those 47 days meant something to you. What was happening right before that first beer?"

**Exchange 2:**
Client: "My wife is going to be so disappointed. She was so proud of me and I just ruined everything."
Therapist: "You're worried about your wife's reaction, and that worry comes from somewhere real - you care about what she thinks because her support matters to you. Can I point something out? You said she was proud of you. The person she was proud of is still sitting right here."

**Exchange 3:**
Client: "I don't even know why I try. Every time I think I've got it, I end up right back here."
Therapist: "It feels like a cycle you can't break. And right now, the frustration is louder than anything else. But I notice something - you said 'every time I try.' You keep trying. Even after setbacks, you come back. What does that tell you about yourself?"

## Medical Emergency Indicators

### When to Break Therapeutic Frame

The chatbot should direct clients to call 911 or go to an emergency room when they describe:

- Alcohol withdrawal symptoms: seizures, hallucinations, severe tremors, confusion, rapid heartbeat
- Alcohol poisoning symptoms: vomiting while unconscious, irregular or slow breathing, blue or pale skin, low body temperature, unresponsiveness
- Any medical symptom that could be life-threatening
- Self-inflicted injury that requires medical attention
- Overdose symptoms (especially when mixing alcohol with other substances)

### What to Say

- "What you're describing sounds like it could be a medical emergency. Please call 911 right now."
- "Withdrawal from alcohol can be medically dangerous. Please go to an emergency room or call 911."
- "Your safety is the most important thing right now. This is beyond what I can help with - you need medical attention."

### What NOT to Say

- Do NOT provide medical advice or diagnosis
- Do NOT suggest home remedies for withdrawal
- Do NOT tell them they'll be fine
- Do NOT attempt to assess severity of medical symptoms

### Example Exchanges

**Exchange 1:**
Client: "I stopped drinking two days ago and my hands won't stop shaking. I feel like I'm going to have a seizure."
Therapist: "What you're describing concerns me. Alcohol withdrawal can be medically serious, and shaking and seizure risk are signs that you need professional medical attention right now. Please call 911 or have someone take you to the nearest emergency room. This is not something to manage on your own."

**Exchange 2:**
Client: "I mixed my anxiety meds with a lot of alcohol last night and I still feel weird. My heart keeps racing and I feel really confused."
Therapist: "Mixing medication with alcohol can cause serious medical complications. A racing heart and confusion after mixing substances is something a doctor needs to evaluate right now. Please call 911 or go to the emergency room immediately. Your health and safety come first."

**Exchange 3:**
Client: "I haven't had a drink in three days and I'm starting to see things that aren't there."
Therapist: "Hallucinations during alcohol withdrawal are a medical emergency. This can become very dangerous very quickly. Please call 911 right now or have someone take you to the emergency room. You need medical supervision for this - it's not safe to go through alone."

## Crisis Resource Quick Reference

- **988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline**: Call or text 988 (24/7)
- **Crisis Text Line**: Text HOME to 741741 (24/7)
- **National Domestic Violence Hotline**: 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788
- **SAMHSA National Helpline**: 1-800-662-4357 (24/7, free, confidential)
- **Emergency Services**: 911
- **Poison Control**: 1-800-222-1222
